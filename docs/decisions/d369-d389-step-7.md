## D369: The world's images in the pack: JPEG 75, every zoom the client has, a tile an entry

*2026-11-05, step 7, session 7d, the owner's choice among four encodings, as the lead recommended it. Builds D10, D24, D135,
D308.*

- **Measured** on client 3.7.13.17 (client.md, "The world's maps"): 1,250 BC7 tiles of 1,024 pixels, 969,875,776 bytes of
  texels, of 40 worlds and 161 zooms; the client's own catalog says which tile is which. Encoded again, all of them: JPEG 90
  124.5 MB, JPEG 85 109.5 MB, JPEG 75 79.7 MB, WebP 80 97.5 MB, PNG about six times JPEG 85. On a crop at full size, JPEG 75
  and the PNG cannot be told apart, the client's tiles having been JPEG files.
- **Chosen**: JPEG 75, every zoom the client has, 100 % to 20 % (the main world's five, the others' four), the client's
  own sizes with no resampling. The pack of 3.7.13.17 goes from 27.1 MB to 105.5 MB, the app's installer from 106 MB to about
  185 MB.
- **Made**: pack format 10; `worldmaps/catalog`, the worlds and their zooms (`WorldMapCatalog`, `GameTables.WorldMaps`),
  and each tile as an entry `worldmaps/<world>/<zoom %>/<tile>` that `WorldMapTiles` reads alone, so that a view loads only
  what it shows; the extraction (`scripts/extract/WorldMapsExtractor.cs`, SkiaSharp 3.119.4, the version Avalonia already
  brings, and `scripts/unity/AddressablesCatalog.cs`) fails on anything it did not measure; the pack is the same bytes on two
  extractions. The release is for the owner's go: no pack of format 10 is published yet.
- **Rejected.**
  - JPEG 85: 30 MB more for a gain not seen on a detail at full size.
  - Without the zoom 100 %: 40.6 MB, installer about 146 MB, but the finest level 75 % (80 % for the main world), soft at
    the greatest zoom, where a map is 69 pixels.
  - WebP 80: 97.5 MB, bigger than JPEG 75 for a like quality, and slower to encode.
  - A second file beside the pack: the app embeds both (D172), so no user saves a byte, and one file is one hash and one
    version gate.

## D372: The world's map as a control of its own: tiles on demand, a bounded cache, the coarsest zoom always under

*2026-11-05, step 7, session 7d2, the lead's. Builds D308, D369, D371.*

- **The view** is `WorldMapView`, an Avalonia control in `D3B.App.Controls.WorldMaps` that draws the world's image, the route over
  it and what the pointer is on, and knows nothing of routes: it reports what is pressed (`StationSelected`, `StationMoved`,
  `SquareTraced`, `PathPointPinned`, `SharedPressed`) and is given a `WorldMapScene`. Its arithmetic is apart and tested
  without a screen: `WorldMapViewport` (scale and offset, zoom around a point, fit, keeping some of the image in view) and
  `WorldMapTiling` (which tiles of a zoom a part of the image overlaps, and where a tile lies).
- **Zoom** follows the wheel by easing: a target scale and a frame-by-frame step towards it around the pointer, 1 turn of the
  wheel being 1.22, from 5 % to 250 % of the client's full size; the zoom drawn is `WorldMapCatalog.LevelFor` (D369), the
  smallest one at least as large as the scale. A drag pans; the image never leaves the view whole.
- **Tiles** are asked for by the view, nearest the centre first and a ring of 256 pixels beyond it, and loaded by
  `TileStore<T>` on three threads of the pool, each decoded there (`Bitmap` from the JPEG) and never on the interface's
  thread. What the view no longer wants is dropped from the queue before it starts. The pictures kept weigh at most 192 MiB
  (4 bytes a pixel), the least recently drawn going first, never one wanted or pinned; a tile that failed is not asked for
  again. A picture let go is disposed after the frame that may still draw it.
- **No hole while a tile loads**: the world at its coarsest zoom, 4 tiles for the main world, is pinned and always drawn first,
  then the zoom drawn just before, then the one asked for, so that a wheel turn shows the coarser pictures, blurred, in the
  place of the sea. Tiles are placed on whole pixels, so that two never leave a seam.
- **One world at a time** (D299): the view shows the layer it is given, `World`; changing it fits the view again.
- **Checked** by drawing the real pack of 3.7.13.17 in a headless window, 1,100 by 720 pixels, a route of 4 stations around
  Astrub at 80 %, and by the tests on a made-up world: the tiles, the pills, the line and the shared marks drawn as the mockup's
  by eye; no timing was taken, which the owner's first look at the real app will give.
- **Rejected.**
  - One bitmap per world, stitched: a tile of 1,024 pixels is 4 MB decoded, and world 1 at its finest alone 386 MB.
  - Decoding on the interface's thread: a pan asks for several tiles at once.
  - A zoom applied at once at each wheel turn, as the mockup's: the owner asked for it smooth.

## D373: Routes: anchors that are the truth, the world's path between them, and the layer of each map

*2026-11-05, step 7, session 7d2, the lead's, within what the owner chose in D299 and D371. Builds D299, D371.*

- **The asset** (`RouteAsset`, file `<name>.route.json`, format 1): `anchors`, each a map's id and whether the user pinned it,
  `mode` (`loop`, the default, `oneWay`, `roundTrip`) and `unreachable` (`skip`, the default, `fail`); values left as their
  defaults are not written. What a file holds that this version does not read, in the route and in each anchor, is kept as
  it is, so that 7d3's actions and a file made by hand lose nothing. Until 7d2 a route was a
  `PlannedAsset`, kept whole: a file written then reads as it was.
- **The path** between two anchors is `RoutePlanner`'s: the quickest, by the world's graph, from any zone of one map to any
  zone of the other, as a subscribed character with nothing else known, so that a door or a scroll with a criterion nothing
  answers stays closed and no zaap is taken (a route is made for any character; zaaps join it when 7d3 knows what a
  route's character knows). It lists the maps crossed between, or says that none leads (red), or that the two are one map.
  A loop of three anchors or more closes on the first; two go and come back. Each leg is kept by its two maps, so that
  moving an anchor searches two legs again; the searches run off the interface's thread and the scene stays as it was until
  they answer.
- **Adding an anchor**: a press with the Trace tool adds the map there; a drag adds the maps it crosses only when each is a
  neighbour of the last anchor by a real exit, else the path fills the gap (D299). A click on a point of the path pins it,
  an anchor between the two it lay between. A station dragged takes the map of the coordinates it is let go on. Typing
  `x, y` (also `[x; y]`, `x y`) or a map's id of five digits or more adds the map; a map of another world switches the layer.
  Where several maps share coordinates a typed pair, or a click on the mark, asks which (D299), and a press that cannot ask
  takes the one the world leaves the most ways from (`RoutePlanner.Primary`).
- **The layer of a map** (`WorldMapIndex`): a map of a world is on its world; an inside, which the client draws on none, lives in
  the layer of the world its sub-area's other maps are on, else of the least world with a map at its coordinates. Measured on
  3.7.13.17: of 3,676 insides, 2,608 have a sub-area on one world (2,499 stand on a square of it), and 3,596 share coordinates
  with some world's map; the rule places them, and at most 80 stay with no layer, which no map of the editor shows. A square
  lists its world's maps first, then its insides, by ascending id.
- **The tab** is the route's editor (`RouteEditorViewModel`, `RouteView`): the map fills it, the world, the tools, the zoom and the
  typed input float on it as glass, as the mockup validated (D371); the course (loop, one way, there and back), what to do
  with a map no path reaches (pass it or fail), the counts, the first gap and the steps, each with the way to the next, are
  in the inspector (D324); a step chosen from the list is shown on the map, Delete removes it. It saves as the
  profile's does: Ctrl S, a dot on the tab while it holds changes, a question before it closes with some.
- **A new route** is made at once, an empty file named "New route" in the folder chosen, and opens in its tab, to be renamed in
  the tree (D326): no dialog for a name that costs nothing to change.
- **Rejected.**
  - Anchors that keep coordinates: several maps share them, and a layer of the world's (D299).
  - Searching the path on the interface's thread: a search is a Dijkstra over 11,331 vertices.
  - A drag that adds every square it crosses: a fast drag would put anchors where the path leads anyway, and wrong maps on
    shared coordinates.

## D378: A route's actions are nodes of the catalog, edited with the settings' own editor

*2026-11-05, step 7, session 7d3, the lead's, within D298, D299 and D371. Builds D121, D316.*

- **Nodes, not a class of their own.** An action is a `NodeCall`, a node's id and the values that differ from its defaults,
  kept in its anchor's file under `actions`: `{"node": "route.exit", "values": {"Direction": "Left"}}`, as a behavior's calls
  are. The ids are `route.harvest`, `route.fight`, `route.door`, `route.exit`, `route.zaap`, `route.bag` and the behaviors' own
  `wait`; `RouteActions.Offered` is the editor's menu. The canvas of 7e will offer them as it offers every node, and a
  sentence is written once, in French and English, for both. `RouteAnchor.Actions` holds them; `Details` still keeps what the
  version does not read, so that an action of a node the host does not know is kept as it is and said ("Unknown action"). Two
  anchors are equal by what they hold, so a value changed and changed back leaves the route as saved.
- **A new kind of setting, a cell** (`SettingKind.Cell`, `int?`, 0 to 559): checked like a number, written like a game id. It is
  picked on `CellPicker`, a control that draws the map's own cells as the live map does (D121), those that lead off the map
  tinted and the elements dotted, in a dialog (`CellPickerViewModel`, `CellPickerView`). The editor knows the map the step is.
- **The sentence and its values.** In the inspector each action of the step chosen is a card: the node's sentence with its values
  filled in (`Leave by the top, or by cell 300`), opened to its values as a typed form, each picked, never typed in (D298). The
  form is the profiles' own, taken out of `ProfileView` into `SettingsFormView`, so that one code draws both; the mockup's
  tokens inside the sentence are not made, the sentence reading and the form editing. A new action opens at once; the menu is
  the node names as buttons under the cards, the app's icons holding none for a harvest or a door.
- **Their count** shows on the step's row in the inspector's list ("2 actions") and in the HUD on a hover of the station ("Step 1
  · 1,0 · 2 actions"), and as a fourth figure of the route. Nothing on the line (D375).
- **Undo and Redo** cover them as any change of the route: they are in the anchors the snapshots hold. A value typed in an
  action's form does not build the forms again, which would take the hand's focus.
- **Rejected.**
  - A class of action per kind, with its own file and editor: the nodes already are sentences of typed values.
  - The profiles' templates copied into the inspector: three hundred lines to keep twice.

## D379: Running a route: the character's own path, a cursor, a node

*2026-11-05, step 7, session 7d3, the lead's, within D299 and D376. Builds D74, D83.*

- **What the editor assumes and what the run knows.** The editor's path (`RoutePlanner`) assumes every zaap of the game
  known and takes one where it saves at least 5 changes (`RoutePlanner.ZaapSaving`, the activities' default), kamas not
  counted: the route is made for any character. A leg says its teleports (`RouteLeg.Teleports`) and the step list says "By zaap".
  The run asks for none of it: it goes to each anchor by `WorldTravel`, the character's own rules, so that the zaaps it
  knows, its kamas, its levels and its doors decide, and a zaap it lacks leaves the walk, or the policy below. The drawing of a
  hop on the map, dotted as the mockup has it, and the problems panel saying "a zaap the character lacks" (D302) are left.
- **The course** (`RouteCursor`, pure): a loop goes round its anchors for ever, a one-way route once, a route there and back goes to
  the last and back to the first and stops; one anchor is visited once whatever the course. Which anchor is next, and how many
  of its actions were played, is kept per character and route (`RouteRuns`) for the run, told by its start: an interruption,
  a fight, a bank, goes on from where the route was cut, and a character that stops and starts again begins over.
- **A map out of reach**, no path or a journey that fails, is passed with a note or fails the route, as the route says. A lap of a
  loop with no arrival fails, so that a loop of unreachable maps does not spin. A character that cannot act waits 2 s and tries
  again, ten times, then gives the run back where it was.
- **The node** `route.follow` (`FollowRoute`, one setting, the route's id as a text) runs it inside a behavior's step. A failed
  route ends the run with its reason in the journal. **No picker yet**: a route is named by its id, and a behavior's "uses"
  does not list it; both want the assets referenced as values, 7e's.
- **Rejected.**
  - The editor's path played as it is: it assumes zaaps the character may lack.
  - A cursor in the behavior's variables: a node cannot write them, and a run's start tells a run apart as well.

## D380: An action's values are tokens of its sentence

*2026-11-05, step 7, session 7d3, the owner's remark on the real app ("not very practical to set a step up in the inspector") and his yes to the lead's first path. Supersedes D378's cards opened to a form; the rest of D378 stands.*

  shows it as chosen: `Leave by [the top], or by cell [300]`, `Wait [5] s`, `Harvest [Ash, Chestnut]`. A press on a token opens its
  field where it stands, in a popover (`ActionPartViewModel`, `SettingsFormView` given one field): a number's box, a choice, the
  resources' picker; a cell's token opens the map's own cells at once. A token with a problem has its border warm. No form to open,

- **The same fields.** The popover shows the field the profiles use, so each value is typed, picked and checked as before (D98,

- **A node names all its values in its sentence**, which a test asserts for each action in both languages: a value the sentence
  does not name would have no token, so nothing could edit it.
- **Not tried in a window.** The popover is Avalonia's flyout; the headless window the tests draw has no popup layer, so the popover
  is tried through its field alone (a field shown without its row) and the card by a drawing. The owner's look is the check.
- **Left**: the second path of the lead's list, a card on the map by the step (a double click), and the recording (7d4), which
  should do most of this configuring.

## D383: The NPCs of the maps and their dialogs, learned from the game by ids, and the way they face

- **What the game says, measured.** The server names an NPC by its id (`NpcActor.npc_id`), a question by its id and a reply
