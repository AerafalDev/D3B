## D394: 7d4 split: what needs no client now (undo, keys, dragging, zones), recording and Simplify after the measure in game

*2026-11-09, step 7, session 7d4, the owner's choice among three splits, with the lead's recommendation. Amends D376; builds D299, D375.*

- **The split.** D376 gave 7d4 four pieces. The recording reads what the host really emits (a map left by an exit, a door, a zaap, the
  haven bag, an NPC) and can only be checked on a client; D377 and the status page already ask for a measure in game with the owner
  before 7e grows the code further. So this session makes the three pieces that need no client, and **recording while playing and
  Simplify become 7d4b**, after that measure. Rejected: the zones alone (leaves the two small pieces to a session each), and all four in one
  branch (the recording could not be tried, the pull request would be very wide).
- **Undo in the other editors.** The list of steps (`SequenceEditorViewModel`) and the profile's editor (`ProfileViewModel`) are
  `IUndoable`, so Edit's Undo and Redo, Ctrl Z, Ctrl Y and the command search follow them as they follow the route's. One class holds
  the history, `UndoHistory<T>` (200 states, an undo ends a run): a state is recorded at each change, and changes that follow each
  other with the same *run* are one to take back. A number or a text typed in a field is one run per field (`SettingsEditor.TypingRun`,
  from the field that changed last, `LastEdited`): a word is not a step per letter, and another field is another step; a name typed in
  is the run "name"; a gesture on the map is the run of the press. The profile's history is its name, its activity and its settings
  as written; it starts when the editor opens and ends when it closes. The route editor keeps its own snapshots (D375), working and tested: moving it onto `UndoHistory` is
  not worth a change of its own.
- **Steps reordered by dragging.** One behavior, `ReorderDrag` (`D3B.App.Controls`), attached to an `ItemsControl` with a command that is
  given a `ReorderRequest(From, To)`: a press that moves 4 pixels picks a row up (a press on a field typed in is left to it, a press
  that does not move is the click it was), a line above or below the row under the pointer says where it falls, the drop calls the
  command. The model changes and builds its rows again, so nothing is moved in the control. It serves the route's steps in the inspector
  (`MoveStep`, which the Alt arrows now use too), the actions of a step (`MoveAction`) and the steps of a list (`MoveTo`). There is no
  scroll while dragging near an edge, and no drag between lists.
- **Keys of the other editors.** The same behavior gives a row the focus (Tab) and moves it with Alt and the up and down arrows, and
  removes it with Delete in the lists that have a remove command (the actions of a step, the steps of a list); the focus follows the row
  that moved. The list moves the focus by the arrows and handles them before the behavior, so it listens to events already handled. The
  profile's "more" button, the only one without a name for a screen reader, has one; the others of the sequence and the profile editors already had.
- **Zones.** A zone is a file `<name>.zone.json` (`ZoneAsset`, `D3B.Studio.Assets`): the sub-areas whose every map it holds, the maps it
  names, the maps it leaves out, ids ascending; what else the file holds is kept (`Details`, as a route's). It holds the maps of its
  sub-areas and the maps named, less those left out (`ZoneMaps.Resolve` and `Holds`). The ids are the maps', never coordinates, which several
  maps share (D299). `PlannedAsset` no longer takes the zone kind.
- **The zones' editor** (`ZoneEditorViewModel`, `ZoneView`, in a tab as the route's) is on the route editor's map control, with four
  tools: *Move* (V) only pans; *Paint* (B) takes the first map of the square pressed or crossed, the world's before the insides
  (`WorldMapIndex`'s order); *Erase* (E) takes out every map held on the square, and a map of a sub-area held whole is left out rather
  than forgotten, so that painting it back is the reverse; *Sub-area* (R) adds the whole sub-area of the map pressed, or takes it
  out when it is held (its maps named and left out forgotten with it), a drag not toggling it again. The squares held are drawn as one
  green shape outlined where no other lies beside (`WorldMapZoneLayer`, the zones' colour of D326), those left out dotted red, the
  insides of a square that holds a world map counting for the square. The layer is one world at a time, as the route's; a list over the map
  names the sub-areas held, each taken out with a press. The map's shared-coordinates marks are off (painting takes the world's map; the
  insides come with their sub-area or by `Add`). Undo and Redo cover it, a press and its drag one change. The inspector shows the zone
  as a generic asset: a panel of its figures is not made.
- **What this leaves.** Typing a map's id or coordinates into the zone, as the route's box does, and a *use* of zones: nothing reads a zone
  yet but `ZoneMaps`, the nodes and conditions of 7f being the ones to (D298). The recording and Simplify (7d4b).
- **Rejected.**
  - A history per editor, written again each time: the three would diverge on what a run is.
  - A drag handle on every row: the rows are cards of buttons, a handle would be a third control to find; the keys and the buttons stay for those who do not drag.
  - A zone as a list of coordinates: a square holds several maps (D299).
