# Code of conduct

D3B is made by people with different backgrounds, languages and levels of experience: players who have never opened a
pull request, developers who have never played Dofus, and everyone between. This code says how we treat each other in
the project's spaces, so that all of them can take part.

## What we expect

- **Be kind and patient.** Assume good faith, and remember that the person on the other side may be new to open
  source, to .NET or to English.
- **Discuss the work, not the person.** Disagree with reasons and, where you can, with measurements: a capture
  described by `scripts/capture.cs`, a bench run, a fight replayed. Say what is wrong and why, not who is wrong.
- **Accept a no with its reasons.** Decisions are recorded with their reasons in `docs/decisions.md`. Any of them can be
  revisited with new evidence; the same request repeated without it cannot.
- **Help newcomers find their way.** Point to the right document rather than telling someone to read the docs.
- **Credit others' work**, including the ideas you took from another project.

## What is not accepted

- Harassment, insults, threats, and remarks or jokes about anyone's origin, gender, sexual orientation, disability,
  age, religion, appearance, language or level of experience.
- Sexual language, imagery or advances.
- Trolling, personal attacks, and repeatedly derailing a discussion.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent. In a project about a game, that includes account
  names, character names, captures, login tickets and anything else that identifies a player.
- Using the project's spaces to target anyone, including other players, Ankama's staff and its moderators.
- Sharing stolen accounts or credentials, leaked or cracked code of other bots, or any other material you have no right
  to share.
- Advertising bots, accounts, kamas or services for sale.

## Scope

This code applies in every space of the project: issues, pull requests, reviews, commits and their comments, and
anywhere a person speaks for D3B in public.

## Reporting

If someone's conduct harms you or others, report it privately to the maintainer, Aerafal
([AerafalDev](https://github.com/AerafalDev) on GitHub), through the repository's private reporting form: on the
[Security](https://github.com/AerafalDev/D3B/security) tab, choose *Report a vulnerability* and start its title with
"Conduct". Only the maintainer reads it. Say what happened, where, with links to the comments involved, and whether you
want to stay anonymous towards the person you report.

If the report is about the maintainer, or the maintainer does not answer, use GitHub's own
[report abuse form](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam),
which reaches GitHub's staff.

Reports are kept confidential. Nobody is penalized for reporting in good faith.

## Enforcement

The maintainer reviews every report, hears the person involved, and responds in proportion to the harm:

1. **A note**, private or public, explaining what was wrong and what is expected instead.
2. **A warning**, stating the consequences of a repetition.
3. **A temporary block** from interacting with the project.
4. **A permanent block.**

Severe cases, such as threats or publishing someone's private information, go straight to a permanent block and are
reported to GitHub. Comments, commits and other content that break this code may be edited, hidden or removed, with the
reason given when that is safe. Whoever is sanctioned may answer the decision once, with new facts.
